# Authority and approval

Read this leaf only when a caller, child agent or tool can request access to a protected resource, or when an effect needs independent human confirmation. The [trust and privacy chapter](trust-and-privacy.md) owns the threat model; the [action and tool boundary](../../agent-harness-model-and-tools/references/action-and-tool-boundary.md) owns execution and truthful effect results. Follow [system flow 4](../../agent-system-engineering/references/system-flows.md#4-human-approval-of-an-effect) for the hand-off.

## Authority at the effect

For owner-scoped resources, apply the canonical owner predicate to individual reads, list queries and archive mutations; filter before pagination and before mutation. Check an owner's allowed list and archive alongside another principal's attempts, including aliases and unavailable owner resolution; a failed resolution must not broaden the query or change a record.

When several entry selectors are supported, specify deterministic precedence before resolution, validate each supplied selector's type and consistency, and refuse malformed or ambiguous selections rather than silently switching entries. Test competing selectors, explicit and absent values, and an invalid preferred selector; authorization must bind to the entry actually selected.

When credential class controls attribution or access, derive class and charge scope from verified metadata. **Design requirement:** validate metadata types before membership checks and map malformed shapes to a fixed refusal; this stronger behavior is proposed and requires verification. Define each class's bindings and any intentional fall-through to an ordinary class; malformed or mixed shapes are unknown. Enforced policy refuses unknown privileged requests; shadow assessment may record the violation and continue and is not an enforcement result. Exercise malformed arrays and objects, unmatched metadata, valid ordinary fall-through and scope mismatch in both modes.

Identify the authenticated principal, resource, operation and applicable resource or policy version from trusted evidence. A request can nominate a resource or propose an operation; that nomination is not proof of permission. Resolve the target and check both membership or entitlement and object ownership where the policy requires both. Reject a mismatch rather than repairing it with a default scope. A missing or unavailable verifier is not an allow; distinguish a retryable inability to decide from an authoritative denial so callers do not mistake an outage for revocation.

Carry only the verified authority needed by the next boundary. In a synchronous process this can be an isolated request context; when a task crosses a queue or process boundary, persist or otherwise protect a versioned authority record and have the worker reconstruct it from the trusted record. Treat queue fields, model arguments and caller-supplied headers as untrusted assertions. At the effect owner, re-check the binding and current authority according to the declared freshness and revocation policy. A signed token is useful only if its signature, issuer, audience, lifetime and relevant claims are validated by the receiving boundary. A service credential authenticates the service under its grant; it does not automatically establish an end user's identity or permission.

For a resource named by a caller, resolve its authoritative binding, then check every applicable entitlement and ownership predicate before reading protected content. Set the binding and required grant before issuing a credential for a new entry; reject malformed, unavailable or ambiguous resolution without inventing a default. Define whether disabling an entry revokes existing grants and how frequently that decision is refreshed. If a copied or historical binding cannot authorize a new resource, distinguish it from a fresh binding and refuse reuse. Preserve the resolved scope across simultaneous sessions instead of rebinding a shared credential to whichever request arrived last. Test absent and forged scope, mismatched owners, disabled entry, resolver outage and concurrent sessions. Different errors for missing and forbidden resources can reveal existence; choose that disclosure explicitly.

When anonymous access exists, an anonymous credential proves only the verified anonymous principal and its granted scope, not a signed-in identity. When old credentials remain supported, write a compatibility matrix for each version's scope source and checks, reject unrecognized or incomplete versions, and remove the legacy branch only after its authorized lifetime ends. Requiring explicit expiry and exact claim types is a design requirement when a bounded lifetime and unambiguous identity class are required; verify these at the actual verifier. For embedded origin proofs, the issuer binds its registered origin and selected entry to the proof; each participating boundary verifies its relevant proof and binding before acting. Test substituted origin, entry, issuer and expired proof at both sides; a browser origin or bearer proof alone is not human authentication. Choose proof lifetime from exposure and renewal needs.

For a service-origin run, bind principal, allowed capability, target, scope and effective policy version together; reject invalid combinations and avoid inventing an end user. A typed authority shape is not sufficient until the executor verifies compatibility with the actual target and policy. A worker retrieves credentials through authorized custody for the bound principal and stops if a required credential is absent or expired. A task's stable identity and its attempt generation are distinct: a stale generation cannot publish an authoritative terminal result. Test omitted scope on pinned and unpinned targets separately; removing a global fallback does not prove a pinned target rejects missing request scope. Key readback, when present, also binds key identity, purpose, scope and canonical owner.

## Choosing a carrier

| Premise and pressure | Minimal choice | Extension and obligation |
|---|---|---|
| One trusted principal, local call, no protected cross-principal resource | Process-owned capability and explicit allowed operations | Add an identity protocol only when a distinct principal or resource boundary appears. |
| Several callers or protected resources | Verify identity and resolve resource scope at admission and enforcement | Maintain entitlement and ownership checks separately; a valid identity alone grants neither. |
| Work crosses a process or outlives admission | Protected authority record referenced by a task identity | Define expiry, revocation and policy-version semantics and verify them at the receiver. |
| A child or remote tool acts on a caller's behalf | Explicit attenuated grant and authenticated recipient | Bind allowed resource and operations, expiry and any delegation limit; enforce at each effect owner. |
| Independent approval is required | Server-controlled approval bound to the complete proposal | Consume once atomically with the effect, or use an equivalent qualified effect-owner protocol; otherwise refuse. |

Choose the smallest authority carrier that satisfies the actual principal and effect boundary: distributed grants add custody, expiry, revocation and receiver-availability work, while independent confirmation also interrupts the user and binds execution to one exact proposal. This is a recorded design judgment, not a measured friction estimate. Use confirmation where policy requires it; keep authorized routine operations bounded without inventing another approval step. When current verification is indispensable, its outage costs availability rather than granting permission.

## Delegation and credentials

Permit a missing credential or reduced tool requirement only for a trusted origin under an explicitly named policy; never infer that downgrade from a request or model proposal. Check a trusted permitted origin and an untrusted request proposing the same downgrade: the latter is refused.

For each authenticated destination decide user-token forwarding and request-context-header forwarding separately, including external destinations and discovery calls. An allowed connection does not imply either disclosure is allowed. Test all four combinations and a forbidden recipient; no unapproved token or context header should cross the boundary.

When related credential sets form one logical handoff, provision and publish them as a consistent unit or with a committed manifest that prevents partial reads. Define whether reads consume or retain each set, expiry, children and retry access, and cleanup after completion. If any required set is missing or expired, stop rather than execute with partial authority. Check interrupted provisioning, concurrent child reads, retries and expiry; each reader gets a consistent complete set or a safe failure.

Delegate the smallest authority needed for the child outcome, never a broader grant than the parent actually holds and policy permits it to pass on. Bind the grant to the child or recipient, resource, operation, constraints, expiry and relevant version. The receiver checks those facts independently; the child's prose and its asserted lineage do not enlarge the grant. A child result is evidence, not a new instruction or authorization. If a child fails after a possible effect, reconcile that effect before retry; replay only under verified idempotency for the same operation and body, otherwise stop for manual reconciliation.

For remote tools, authenticate the destination before forwarding a user credential. Allowlist intended recipients and header names, protect reserved authority fields from custom-header collisions, and derive scope from the trusted record after any merge. A connection credential and an end-user credential serve different principals and must not substitute for one another. If a credential is missing, expired or points at another principal, fail the protected operation without exposing the credential in the error. For concurrent executions isolate request context and clear it on success, failure, cancellation and early stream exit; verify that the next execution cannot inherit the previous principal.

Inventory each carrier's issuer, receiver, grant, lifetime, authorized readers and verification location, including tool discovery as well as execution. Preserve base service credentials against case-insensitive header collisions; remove optional authority fields when absent from the sealed scope rather than letting a stale field survive. Treat an unset allowlist, an explicitly empty list and an invalid entry according to a written fail-safe policy; check origin normalization for scheme, effective port, userinfo, lookalike hosts and equivalent duplicate spellings. Discovery that reuses a run client may transmit user credentials even if standalone discovery does not; use a separate policy when that is unacceptable. A user-scoped call must not fall back to service authority when the user credential is absent.

Where a receiver's identity middleware applies only to declared tools, inspect every effective execution path, including tools without that declaration; a token-subject comparison without signature verification is consistency checking, not authorization. For actions forbidden to delegated work, interpret malformed or missing lineage under the selected fail-safe policy at the actual enforcement point; different parsers can disagree. Forward only permitted child fields, reconstruct child authority from the verified parent, and test forged parent references and cross-run substitution.

For service credentials, define issuer, audience or recipient, permissions, secure generation, rotation and revocation. Credential length alone does not establish entropy. If rotating with an overlap set, test old and new keys, remove the retired key, and check revocation after every receiver refreshes, including a failed refresh; a nominal refresh interval is not a fleet revocation guarantee. Compare secret values safely and distinguish per-candidate timing from the behavior of a whole set. Machine-only export, when required, needs authorization, explicit opt-in and audit before disclosure. A service-wide key does not imply per-tool authorization; per-caller keys, scoped tokens or stricter target pinning are design choices to implement and verify when the threat warrants them, not automatically built guarantees.

When comparing bearer credentials, validate and encode supported input safely before constant-time byte comparison; malformed or unexpected Unicode must yield a controlled refusal rather than an exception. Inspect authentication objects and their representations as well as logs and errors, because a library can render the bearer even when the comparison is safe. Exercise non-ASCII and malformed inputs and a synthetic secret through rendering; expect refusal without disclosure.

## Identity transitions

When guest work must follow a signed-in principal, bind one source identity to one verified target and define the scope of reassignment. Serialize conflicting merges, persist a target pin and operation state, and recheck the pin and claim before applying a later phase. A repeated request for the same completed merge should return its recorded outcome; a conflicting target must not take ownership. Define lock order across merge, deletion and resolution, and fence late writes from the retired identity. If a one-time credential is consumed before merge confirmation, make the recovery path explicit rather than assuming the credential can be replayed. Distinguish a retired identity from an unavailable retirement check in each route's client contract, without admitting an unverified principal.

Check concurrent conflicting targets, same-target failure and retry, a paused stale second phase, and a late write from the retired principal. Expect exactly one canonical owner and no cross-owner data; if the result cannot be established, stop for reconciliation. Omit this machinery when no identity transition exists. Lifetime and retry constants require workload and recovery evidence, not copied defaults.

## Authentication abuse

When a public sign-in accepts a short one-time code, expiration alone does not prevent repeated or distributed guesses. Require or independently qualify attempt protection at its real owner, including per-session attempts and account issuance or resend limits as applicable; per-address limiting alone is insufficient. Check exhaustion, resend, multiple addresses and upstream outage without assuming an unverified upstream control exists. Omit these controls when that authentication mechanism is absent.

## Independent approval

**Design requirement:** when policy requires independent confirmation, present the exact canonical proposal, including effect-defining values, actor, operation, resource, applicable version and expiry, to an authenticated person authorized to approve it. Bind the server-issued approval to all of these, to the approving principal and to a stable operation identity. A client assertion, a model-written boolean or an instruction to ask first is not independent confirmation.

At execution, verify the binding, the approver's authority, the current version and expiry. Atomically consume the approval with the effect, or use an equivalent effect-owner protocol that prevents reuse and guarantees the approved effect is the one performed. If the resource or proposal changes, obtain a new approval. The effect owner returns a durable outcome receipt bound to the stable operation identity; the action boundary verifies its issuer and binding through the trusted owner interface. If the action owner cannot establish consumption or the effect outcome, return unknown and reconcile; an approval receipt alone does not establish that an effect happened. The [action boundary](../../agent-harness-model-and-tools/references/action-and-tool-boundary.md) owns operation identity, intent and outcome receipts.

## Causal checks

- **Premise:** a named resource requires both entitlement and ownership. **Symptom:** a valid user can access another person's resource. **Cause and evidence:** a membership check alone establishes only a broader grant, not ownership; this is a design failure mode. **Rule:** enforce each predicate against the resolved resource. **Check:** exercise an owned resource and another person's resource in the same scope; the former succeeds and the latter has no effect. **Does not apply when:** the resource is intentionally shared under a policy granting that access.
- **Premise:** scope is projected across a tool connection. **Symptom:** a caller changes the target by supplying a colliding header. **Cause and evidence:** a lower-trust carrier has overridden a trusted scope; this is a design failure mode. **Rule:** project protected fields from verified authority after merging and reject conflicting reserved names. **Check:** attempt both overwrite and omission, including a scope with no optional field; the receiver sees only the authorized scope. **Does not apply when:** there is no such transport or caller-controlled carrier.
- **Premise:** an effect requires human approval. **Symptom:** an approval for one proposal permits a changed or repeated operation. **Cause and evidence:** approval was not bound to the exact effect or was consumed separately; the stronger protocol is a design requirement. **Rule:** bind and consume as above. **Check:** wrong actor, altered body, resource or version, expiry, replay and concurrent consumption all produce no unauthorized effect; a valid approval produces one effect and a verifiable receipt. **Does not apply when:** policy requires no independent approval.

## Build, debug and optimize

If object storage access depends on workload identity, inspect the effective client and resolved principal and exercise the required operations against an authorized fixture; displayed credential metadata alone may be a placeholder. Verify allowed and forbidden operations without exposing credentials, and treat an unverified identity or operation as unknown.

1. **Build:** list principals, protected resources, operations, policy versions, issuing and enforcing owners, and each credential carrier. Check allowed and forbidden combinations in both directions; an untested receiver or a mutable authority field leaves the contract unproven.
2. **Debug:** correlate admission authority, the target resolved at execution and the receiver's authorization decision without recording secrets. A missing value, mismatch or unavailable verifier must remain distinguishable from an allow.
3. **Optimize:** if repeated checks are expensive, select a cache or bounded grant only after defining revocation and freshness. Demonstrate that a revoked grant cannot be renewed by the cache's own healing path; a short cache lifetime alone does not prove a revocation bound.

## Worked example — one implementation under stated premises

Premises: a hosted assistant may edit a user's document; edits require independent confirmation and work can outlive the connection. Admission verifies the caller and stores a protected task record. The assistant proposes an edit, and the interaction boundary presents its canonical body and document version to an authorized reviewer. The document owner verifies a single-use approval against that exact edit and version, consumes it with the mutation, and returns an operation receipt. If the connection drops after dispatch, the host queries the receipt before any replay; an indeterminate effect requires manual reconciliation.
